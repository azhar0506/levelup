# Kubernetes Pod Disruption Budgets

## What are Pod Disruption Budgets

Pod Disruption Budgets (PDBs) are a Kubernetes feature used to maintain application availability during voluntary disruptions.

A PDB defines how many Pods must remain available when Kubernetes or an operator attempts to disrupt Pods.

The keyword here is voluntary.

## What is a Voluntary Disruption

Voluntary disruptions are intentional actions, such as:
•	Node maintenance
•	Cluster upgrades
•	Node draining
•	Cluster autoscaling
•	Manual Pod eviction

PDBs do not protect against:
•	Node crashes
•	Hardware failures
•	Kernel panics

Those are considered involuntary disruptions.

## Why Pod Disruption Budgets Matters

Without a PDB

•	Kubernetes may allow too many Pods to be evicted
•	A drain operation can bring an application entirely offline
•	Replicas alone do not prevent this scenario

PDBs act as a safety guard that prevents disruption beyond a defined threshold.

## PDBs vs Replicas (Important Distinction)

Replicas
•	Ensure availability during normal operation
•	Automatically recreate Pods when they fail
Pod Disruption Budgets
•	Protect availability during voluntary disruptions
•	Can block drain/eviction operations

Replicas replace Pods

PDBs prevent too many Pods from being removed at once

## How PDBs Work (Conceptual)

A Pod Disruption Budget:
•	Selects Pods using labels
•	Defines availability using:
	•	minAvailable or
	•	maxUnavailable

Example logic:
•	5 replicas running
•	minAvailable: 2
•	Kubernetes will block any action that would reduce available Pods below 2

## Behaviour During Node Drains

When draining a node:
	•	Kubernetes checks all applicable PDBs
	•	If evicting a Pod would violate the PDB:
	    •	The eviction is blocked
	    •	The drain command waits or fails

This prevents:
	•	Accidental full outages
	•	Over-disruption of critical workloads


What PDBs Do Not Do

•	Do not guarantee zero downtime
•	Do not replace replicas
•	Do not protect against involuntary failures
•	Do not force Pods to be rescheduled elsewhere

Let's simulate a voluntary disruption. Use the kubectl cordon command to make a node unschedulable, and then delete the pods on that node. ```sh
